Figure 3: The multi-node scheduling design space for one layer. Workload: ResNet-19 layer3_0_conv2 (layer 16) -- KH=3, KW=3, CIN=512, COUT=512, HO=4, WO=4, T=4, 150,994,944 loop iterations of work. Hardware: one fixed LAYS configuration -- 16 nodes, 8 PEs x 4 T-lanes per node (num_pes 32), per-node local buffer 30720/1024/1024 B for weight/psum/vmem, shared global buffer 1966080/65536/65536 B, inter-node fanout at most 16. Bars: the 3,948,846 valid schedules found by exhaustive enumeration (65,379 local-buffer-valid per-dimension node assignments, each crossed with every legal NoC partition), counted into 24 log-spaced latency bins, each shaded by its latency (dark blue = fast, yellow = slow); 24 distinct latencies span 294,912 to 150,994,944 iterations, a 512x spread. Latency is the scheduler's own delay term: the product of every temporally assigned loop factor, equal to the total work divided by the schedule's PE, T-lane and node parallelism. Dashed lines: where the five published architectures land when the real MIP scheduler is run on this same hardware and layer with each architecture's node mapspace (LoAS, PTB at 589,824; GustavSNN, Prosperity, SpinalFlow at 2,359,296 iterations), i.e. 2x and 8x the best schedule this hardware admits.
